\begin{abstract}
We improve the rational certificate for a fixed perturbed
Montgomery--Taylor window by using the square of its kernel at the
total span of nine points. Existing point and derivative data, together
with their certified convexity intervals, suffice: no new kernel table
is computed. Exact difference-constraint closure and 84 closed branches
replace the 47 weakest domains of an existing complete 2,399-domain
cover. A standard-library rational checker verifies the entire cover,
including every finite-box dual residual. The uniform local reward
becomes $805403/10^8$, and the same lossless all-zero transport gives
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T)}{N(T)}
 \ge\frac{66812491}{99194597}=\frac{941021}{1397107}
 =0.673549699486152456\ldots.
\]
The denominator counts all nontrivial zeros with multiplicity; the
numerator counts simple zeros on the critical line. The fixed window,
continuous point/region admission and analytic transport are inherited
from the cited certificates. The new finite implication is checked by
exact rational arithmetic. The strengthened theorem has not yet been
ported to the separate Lean formalization or accepted by the record
website. We make no new zero-free-region claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and inherited inputs}

Let $N(T)$ count all nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ with imaginary part in
$(0,T]$, including multiplicity, and let $N_0^s(T)$ count its simple
critical-line zeros in that interval.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Within the fixed continuous certificate and proved all-zero analytic
framework specified below,
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T)}{N(T)}
 \ge \frac{941021}{1397107}.
\]
The only new computational implication is the complete rational
certificate of Section~\ref{sec:certificate}; no additional correlation
conjecture, zero-free strip or arithmetic moment assumption is used.
\end{theorem}

The previous bound was $66812491/99194740$ \cite{Expanded}. The gain is
\[
 \frac{941021}{1397107}-\frac{66812491}{99194740}
 =\frac{134566003}{138585665617180},
\]
approximately $0.0000970995105451$ percentage points. This comparison
is with the project's fixed preceding theorem, rather than a claim
of exhaustive global priority or official record acceptance.

For reproducibility we specify the unchanged window. Put
$\beta=1/\sqrt2$, $Z=\sqrt2\sin\beta$, and $I=[-1/2,1/2]$.
With zero extension outside $I$, define
\[
 f(u)=\frac{\cos(\sqrt2u)+\sum_{j=1}^{12}c_j\cos(2\pi ju)}{Z},
 \qquad K(t)=\int_I f(u)e^{-2\pi itu}\,du,\qquad w(t)=K(t)^2,
\]
where the integer numerators $10^9c_j$ are
\[
\begin{split}
(&12310798,-15041681,3867664,6489926,-992327,5580097,\\
 &-6846472,3781297,-5670353,3355089,-450523,-218483).
\end{split}
\]
The certified density is positive, even, and has integral one. In
particular $0\le w(t)\le1$ for real $t$. The eight-point functional is
\[
 F_8(h)=\sum_{r=0}^6b_rh_r+
   \sum_{0\le i<j\le7}a_{ij}w(h_i+\cdots+h_{j-1}),
\]
where $10^8b_r=(28898,57272,75526,80958,75526,57272,28898)$,
so $B:=\sum b_r=404350/10^8$. The successive rows of $10^8a_{ij}$ are
\[
\begin{array}{l}
(13630830,0,19565904,20317441,45030671,100000000,200000000),\\
(29997996,30621878,47766489,79682558,109938656,100000000),\\
(37356140,69378121,65335210,79682558,45030671),\\
(38030065,69378121,47766489,20317441),\\
(37356140,30621878,19565904),\quad(29997996,0),\quad(13630830).
\end{array}
\]
There are 26 nonzero pair terms. Each fixed index-span weight sum is
at most two.

\begin{inputclaim}\label{in:fixed}
We retain the fixed public PC8 source \cite{PC8}, the complete capture

\section{The unused total span and valid tangents}

For real gaps $g\in[4/5,\infty)^8$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F9}
 F_9(g)=\tfrac12\{F_8(g_0,\ldots,g_6)+F_8(g_1,\ldots,g_7)\}
             +2w(S),\qquad S=\sum_{r=0}^7g_r.
\end{equation}
The preceding finite certificate imposed only $w(S)\ge0$ on its new
span-eight variable. The original 2,196-point catalog already includes
usable points near the weak configurations' total spans. Convexity
guards, rather than new evaluations, allow us to consume this term.

\begin{lemma}[Interval-valid reanchoring]\label{lem:cut}
Suppose $v\le w(p)$, $d_-\le w'(p)\le d_+$, and $w$ is convex
on an interval containing $l^*:=\min(l,p)$ and $u^*:=\max(u,p)$,
where $l\le u$. Then each of
\[
 v+d_+(l^*-p)+d_-(x-l^*),\qquad
 v+d_-(u^*-p)+d_+(x-u^*)
\]
is at most $w(x)$ for every real $x\in[l,u]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Convexity gives $w(x)\ge v+d(x-p)$ with $d=w'(p)$. The tangent minus
the first line is $(d-d_-)(x-l^*)+(d_+-d)(p-l^*)\ge0$.
For the second it is $(d_+-d)(u^*-x)+(d-d_-)(u^*-p)\ge0$.
The proof also covers points outside the smaller query interval.
\end{proof}

Put $SC=32768$ and $E=10^9$. A catalog entry with integer point $P$
and pack $V+2^{32}D_++2^{64}D_-$ supplies
\[
 p=P/SC,\quad v=V/10^{10},\quad
 d_-=(D_+-2E)/E,\quad d_+=(2E-D_-)/E.
\]
Every new direct-region line is checked against that entry's original
\texttt{PTL} list index and its original convex region. With
$k=(P+16384)\mathbin{\gg}15$ and region endpoints $A_k,B_k$, the
direct guard is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 k<16,\qquad A_k/SC\le\min(l,p)\le p\le\max(u,p)\le B_k/SC,\\
 D_+,D_-\ge1,\qquad D_++D_-\le4E.
\end{gathered}
\]
An alternative old-enabled line must match the point and pack of an
actually enabled old atom and lie inside its already valid interval.
Its inherited \texttt{TVal} assertion supplies the exact tangent lower
bound throughout that interval. The same derivative-interval comparison
in Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} proves both reanchored lines without asserting
convexity beyond an original region.
A value/derivative pack without either full interval guard is rejected.
Closure endpoints are kept as exact rationals; they are not truncated
to grid points. All lines at one physical distance constrain the same
square variable.

\section{Complete closed branches and rational duals}\label{sec:certificate}

The unchanged 482 reflected labels have $482^2$ ordered combinations.
Shared gap bounds leave 4,796; shared spans leave 2,405; full exact
difference-constraint closure leaves exactly 2,399. Negative cycles
certify empty intersections. Prefix coordinates are real: using integer
coefficients in units $5SC$ does not restrict configurations to a lattice.
The inherited capture's direction exclusions, all 41 outer/root checks
and reflected half-spaces are retained.

Replace exactly the 47 domains whose inherited lower bound is below
$805403/10^8$. In each, partition its total-span interval at the interior
original region endpoints. Consecutive branches are closed and share
their common endpoint. Their union equals the entire original interval.
Recompute full difference closure after adding each branch's two span
constraints, and use the resulting projections both for the gap box and
for tangent guards. There are 84 branches in total.

For example, labels $424/215$ have
$S\in[12.905426025390625,13.262969970703125]$. Splitting at
the original region endpoint $434048/SC=13.24609375$ yields exact
branch lower bounds approximately $0.008064135118335435$ and
$0.009937463795419915$. The small upper branch is already paid by
the old terms and pressure, without extending convexity beyond its
certified interval. Labels $131/389$ increase from approximately
$0.0080526095277502$ to $0.00811870169978014$. These decimals
illustrate localization only; the proof consumes the stored fractions.

Each branch has eight gap variables and 34 shared square variables,
including $z_{0,8}=w(S)$. Write its constraints $Ax\le b$ and its
complete finite box $L\le x\le U$, with square bounds $[0,1]$.
The integer objective $q^tx$ is exactly $2\cdot10^8F_9$.

\begin{lemma}[Full residual payment]\label{lem:dual}
For any rational $\lambda\ge0$, put $r=q+A^t\lambda$. Every
feasible real $x$ satisfies
\[
 F_9\ge\frac{-\lambda^tb+
    \sum_i r_i\bigl(L_i\text{ if }r_i\ge0;\ U_i\text{ otherwise}\bigr)}
    {2\cdot10^8}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity $q^tx=r^tx-\lambda^tAx$ gives
$q^tx\ge r^tx-\lambda^tb$. Minimize each residual coefficient
over its full box. No exact stationarity is required.
\end{proof}

Numerical optimization proposes the multipliers. The final checker
uses only integers and rational fractions; it rebuilds the complete
compatibility list, each branch closure, every consumed row and the
full residual. The positive tangent support has 2,955 row uses.
The new direct-region catalog contains 541 original points;
old-enabled rows reuse the admitted atom data. The other 2,352 domains retain their
old exact multipliers, and are actually rechecked as well. The minimum
over all 2,399 finite domains is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:min}
 M=\frac{263917374155379049835090394947}
          {32768000000000000000000000000000}
       >\frac{805403}{10^8}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:uniform}
Under Input~\ref{in:fixed}, $F_9\ge c:=805403/10^8$ on the
entire unbounded separated domain $[4/5,\infty)^8$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If one frame has $F_8\ge c_L$, the other has $F_8\ge c_0$, and
\eqref{eq:F9} gives $F_9\ge(c_L+c_0)/2=c$.
Otherwise each frame is in the full reflected low cover. Their actual
shared gaps select a retained compatible domain; its actual total span
lies in a closed branch if the domain was replaced. All physical
squares satisfy its valid cuts and box. Lemma~\ref{lem:dual} and
\eqref{eq:min} give the result. Every boundary is included.
\end{proof}

The finite minimum exceeds $c$, but the unchanged outside-frame
argument supplies only $c$. A further pointwise improvement needs a
stronger outside argument or a wider complete capture; it cannot be
inferred from the finite minimum alone.

\section{Lossless transport and counting semantics}

The pair weights in \eqref{eq:F9} are half the two old frame weights
plus two on span eight. Every index-span capacity is at most two.
The gap fees are
\[
 10^8b'_r=(14449,43085,66399,78242,78242,66399,43085,14449),
 \qquad \sum b'_r=B.
\]
Summing Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} over the $m-8$ nine-point
windows of an ordered separated set gives
\[
 \sum_{i\ne j}w(y_i-y_j)\ge c(m-8)-B\operatorname{span}(y).
\]
Pairs are charged at most twice and gaps at most $B$; for $m\le8$
the right side is nonpositive.

Here we state the remaining inherited analytic steps explicitly to
fix their consumption. The same majorant
\[
 G(u)=\frac{61}{100}
 \{\sinc(\tfrac45(u-1/2))+\sinc(\tfrac45(u+1/2))\}^2
\]
satisfies $G\ge f$, has Fourier support $[-4/5,4/5]$ with zero
boundary values, and has mass at most $61/32<2$ \cite{Lossless,Joint}.
Use $f_\epsilon=f\chi_\epsilon^2/m_\epsilon$ with
$m_\epsilon\to1$ and $d_\epsilon=\|f_\epsilon-f\|_1\to0$.
For fixed small $\epsilon$, the separated Gram norm is below two.
Each square changes by at most $2d_\epsilon$ and total local pair
weight is less than sixteen, so the error is at most
$32d_\epsilon m$.

Choose a maximal disjoint collection of pairs at distance less than
$4/5$ among the $n$ simple critical-line points. The remainder is
separated. The fixed close-pair estimate
$K(t)>134/825$ gives $w(t)>1/40>c$, paying both points of each
pair. Its smoothing fee is at most $4d_\epsilon$.
Convex spectral-trace pinching with
$\varphi_2(t)=t^2-(t-2)_+^2$ therefore yields for their full Gram
matrix $U$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J(U):=\tr\varphi_2(U)-n
 \ge cn-B\operatorname{span}(y)-8c-32d_\epsilon n.
\end{equation}
Empty and short separated remainders are included.

The actual all-zero Hermitian operator $A$ retains all off-line
conjugate-reflection pairs and multiplicities. Its remainder after
the $n$ simple critical-line columns has positive inertia $p$ with
$N-n\ge2p$. The threshold trace argument \cite{Joint,Knaus} gives
\[
 \tr A^2\ge 2(N-n)+\tr\varphi_2(U)=2N-n+J(U).
\]
The unconditional smoothed correlation formula is
$\tr A^2=(C(f_\epsilon)+o(1))N$ \cite{BGST,Lamzouri}, and the
full normalized height span is at most $N+o(N)$. Taking first
$T\to\infty$ for fixed $\epsilon$, then $\epsilon\to0$, in
\eqref{eq:J} gives
\[
 \liminf\frac{n}{N}\ge\frac{2-C(f)-B}{1-c}.
\]
The certified energy of this exact window is
\[
 C(f)=\frac12+\beta\cot\beta+
 \frac{\sum_jc_j^2(1/2-1/(4\pi^2j^2))}{2\sin^2\beta},
 \qquad 2-C(f)>67216841/10^8.
\]
Hence the displayed bound is at least
\[
 \frac{67216841-404350}{10^8-805403}
 =\frac{66812491}{99194597}=\frac{941021}{1397107},
\]
proving Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The inherited dyadic-to-cumulative
argument preserves these count definitions. This proof does not
replace off-line zeros by real Gram points or discard their inertia.

\section{Reproduction and verification status}\label{sec:trust}

The public data are the following two files, together with the
unchanged expanded certificate:
\begin{verbatim}
output/am-ninth-span-certificate.json
output/am-ninth-span-point-catalog.json
\end{verbatim}
From a clean checkout run
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/am_ninth_span_certificate.py --check
\end{verbatim}
The checker uses the Python standard library, checks dependency
identities, reconstructs all 2,399 domains and 84 closed branches,
and includes all residuals. The small catalog contains mathematical
point/region data, not new formalization code. It identifies the
original public source by fixed commit and SHA-256. With that exact
source available, an optional \texttt{--source} argument additionally
reextracts every consumed entry and compares its original list index,
pack and region. The offline run checks a finite rational implication
under the explicitly inherited continuous point/region admission;
a data hash alone does not prove an analytic kernel assertion.

The source's exact identity, completed checks and an independent
mathematical review are archived with this paper. No floating solver
status is accepted as a certificate, and optimized Python must refuse
execution because assertions are essential to the check.

The
\href{https://github.com/lixiang90/RH-Zero-Proportion-Formalization}
{formalization repository} contains the proof of the previous bound,
\[
 66812491/99194740.
\]
Its verification is not automatically
inherited by the new branches and reward. A full Lean port of this
improvement, website resource compliance and official acceptance
remain uncompleted. This manuscript records the strengthened
mathematical certificate and its exact scope as of October 10, 2026.
