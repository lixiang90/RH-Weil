\begin{abstract}
We strengthen both components of the retained nine-point certificate:
its global eight-point floor and its complete compatible-pair cover.
Forty of 241 original captured frames receive replacement rational duals;
one requires four closed branches. Ten compatible-pair domains receive
twenty closed total-span branches. All 2,399 compatible domains are
rechecked with exact rational arithmetic, including the 47 previously
subdivided domains and every retained original dual. The resulting
uniform reward is $805448/10^8$. The unchanged all-zero lossless transport
gives the lower proportion
\[
 p=\frac{66812491}{99194552}=0.6735500050446319\ldots>0.67355
\]
for simple critical-line zeros relative to all nontrivial zeros counted
with multiplicity. The new finite certificate uses only original
certified kernel points and convexity regions. Its mathematical proof
and exact checker are presented here; subsequent Lean and independent
kernel verification are recorded separately. No website acceptance
or exhaustive global priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and fixed framework}

Let $N(T)$ count all nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ in $(0,T]$ with
multiplicity, and let $N_0^s(T)$ count simple zeros on $\Re s=1/2$.
The same superscripts on $N(T,2T)$ denote the interval $(T,2T]$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under the retained proved continuous AM certificate and its lossless
all-zero analytic transport,
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T)}{N(T)}\ge p,
 \qquad
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,2T)}{N(T,2T)}\ge p,
 \quad p=\frac{66812491}{99194552}.
\]
In particular the proportion $67.355\%$ holds for all sufficiently
large heights. Simple-zero inclusion implies the same lower bounds
for distinct critical-line zeros.
\end{theorem}

Here ``under'' identifies the retained proved components of the
certificate, not a new moment or correlation conjecture. We compare
with the project's preceding bound $668124910/991945881$ \cite{Low}:
\[
 100\left(p-\frac{668124910}{991945881}\right)
 =\frac{602982731275}{24598906818510078}
 =0.0000245125824380\ldots
\]
percentage points. The strict crossing is exact:
$p-13471/20000=1251/247986380000>0$.

The unchanged kernel is $K(t)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}f(u)e^{-2\pi itu}\,du$,
with $w(t)=K(t)^2$, where the positive normalized density is
\[
 f(u)=\frac{\cos(\sqrt2u)+\sum_{j=1}^{12}c_j\cos(2\pi ju)}
 {\sqrt2\sin(1/\sqrt2)}.
\]
The coefficient vector $10^9(c_1,\ldots,c_{12})$ is

\section{Complete real cover and joint improvement}\label{sec:cover}

The retained strong capture states that $F_8<\tau$, with
$\tau=805803/10^8$, implies membership in one of 241 closed original
cells or their reflections \cite{PC8,Ninth}. The compatible-pair
geometry contains exactly 2,399 ordered physical domains. Capture,
reflection and compatibility are statements on real gaps; integer
endpoints encode rational inequalities, not a discretization of the
gaps. Equalities on the boundary are included.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:frame}
For every admissible real seven-gap frame,
$F_8\ge\ell:=805094/10^8$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The finite proof on all 241 cells is given in
Section~\ref{sec:finite}. Reflection preserves the functional and
cell constraints. Outside the captured union the contrapositive of
strong capture gives $F_8\ge\tau>\ell$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pair}
Every compatible captured pair satisfies $F_9\ge M$, where
\[
 M=\frac{32992055894668884928258784247}
         {4096000000000000000000000000000}
   =0.008054701146\ldots.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The complete exact reconstruction in Section~\ref{sec:finite}
retains 2,342 original pair duals, 47 previous replacements with 84
closed branches, and ten new replacements with twenty closed branches.
Their indices are disjoint and their union is the complete compatible
domain list. Every real branch is paid; the displayed fraction is the
minimum of the recomputed bounds. The closed adjacent endpoints have
no coverage gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local}
For every admissible real eight-gap frame,
$F_9\ge c:=805448/10^8$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If both subframes lie in the captured union, Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}
gives $F_9\ge M>c$. Otherwise at least one subframe has $F_8\ge\tau$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:frame} pays the other subframe, and the extra square is
nonnegative. Hence
\[
 F_9\ge\frac{\tau+\ell}{2}
       =\frac{1610897}{200000000}
       =c+\frac1{200000000}>c.
\]
Thus both parts of the complete real cover pay the same target.
\end{proof}

\]
Thirty-nine cells have one replacement dual each. Cell 17 uses four
closed contiguous intervals of its first gap, with exact union equal
to the original first-gap interval. All 201 remaining cells keep
their original YA payment. Each problem has 33 physical variables:
seven gaps and 26 kernel squares. The boxes of the squares remain
the full $[0,1]$ intervals. Across these 43 paid problems there are
449 added rows and 1,194 positive dual multipliers.

The ten new pair domains, in ordered cell labels, are
\[
\begin{split}
 &(389,68),(309,148),(69,158),(399,310),(401,310),\\
 &(69,160),(401,309),(68,160),(310,401),(160,69).
\end{split}
\]
Each has two contiguous closed total-span branches. The original
whole-domain inequalities and the branch inequalities determine
the actual closure; there is no independent choice of left and
right kernel values for their shared spans. These problems use 42
variables: eight gaps and 34 physical squares. Their 810 positive
dual multipliers include 730 positive tangent rows.

All added tangents use 425 entries of the original 2,196-point PTL
table and its retained REG convexity regions. Original point indices,
value/derivative packs and source identity are frozen and checked.
No newly sampled analytic data or curvature conjecture is used.
If $v\le w(p)$, $d_-\le w'(p)\le d_+$, and a certified convexity
region contains $[l_*,u_*]$, with
$l_*:=\min(l,p)$ and $u_*:=\max(u,p)$, then for $x\in[l,u]$,
\[
\begin{split}
w(x)&\ge v+d_+(l_*-p)+d_-(x-l_*),\\
w(x)&\ge v+d_-(u_*-p)+d_+(x-u_*).
\end{split}
\]
These are genuine convex tangent bounds with derivative-interval
corrections. The query interval and the anchor both lie in the
certified region. An anchor outside the query interval is never
used without the indicated corrections.

For the finite implication, write $Ax\le b$, $L\le x\le U$ and
the objective numerator as $q^Tx$. For an exact nonnegative
multiplier vector $\lambda$, set $r=q+A^T\lambda$. Then
\[
 q^Tx\ge-\lambda^Tb+
 \sum_i r_i\begin{cases}L_i,&r_i\ge0,\\U_i,&r_i<0.\end{cases}
\]
The bound includes every residual, even if numerically small.
Scaling back gives the rational lower bound for the real functional.
Floating optimization proposes witnesses only; it does not justify
the accepted implication. Negative-cycle closure can discard a
branch only after its exact emptiness proof. Nonempty branches are
all checked with the actual rows, complete boxes and rational signs.

The minimum strengthened frame bound is
\[
 \frac{8442028677986126589157}{1048576000000000000000000}
 =0.00805094592856\ldots>\ell,
\]
at label 40. The minimum pair payment is $M$ at $(406,70)$.
Consequently the current limiting component is the outer case.
The slightly larger exact minimum of the same stored data is
$16891485543266126589157/2097152000000000000000000$;

\section{Counting transport, reproducibility and scope}

The sum of gap pressures is still $B=404350/10^8$. The original
AM energy certificate pays gain $67216841/10^8$. Sliding the
nine-point frame preserves each pair budget at most two, total
pair mass below sixteen, and endpoint cost $8c$. The near-pair
reward exceeds $1/40>c$; the majorant has mass below two.
The smoothing loss remains $32d\varepsilon$. The Hermitian Gram
argument retains off-line reflected pairs and every multiplicity
\cite{Ninth,Lossless}. First take the height limit at fixed smoothing,
then decrease the smoothing parameter. Its proved lossless
transport yields the claimed proportion
\[
 \frac{67216841/10^8-B}{1-c}
 =\frac{66812491}{99194552}.
\]
The same finite inputs yield the dyadic statement; the retained
counting transport yields the cumulative statement. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, including its interpretation in actual zeta
counts rather than a model spectrum.
